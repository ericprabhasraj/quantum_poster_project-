Introduction

Financial market prediction is a formidable challenge due to the noisy, non-stationary, and chaotic nature of time-series data. Traditional classical models often struggle to capture the complex, high-dimensional correlations inherent in market dynamics.

This project pioneers a hybrid quantum-classical approach to forecast the direction of the FTSE 100 index. Our core objective is to classify future market movements (Up or Down) by leveraging quantum computation to uncover hidden periodicities and patterns that are inaccessible to classical methods alone, aiming for superior predictive accuracy.

Methodology & Workflow

We propose a novel forecasting model that integrates classical data preprocessing with quantum computational layers. This hybrid architecture is designed to harness the strengths of both paradigms for a more robust prediction model.

1. Classical Preprocessing

FTSE 100 daily returns are cleaned, normalized, and windowed.

⮟
2. Quantum Feature Extraction (QFT)

Quantum Fourier Transform is applied to data windows to extract frequency-domain features (hidden cyclical patterns).

⮟
3. Variational Quantum Classifier (VQC)

A parameterized quantum circuit is trained on the QFT features to learn the classification boundary.

⮟
4. Classical Prediction & Post-processing

Measurement outcomes from the VQC are interpreted as "Up" or "Down" market trend predictions.

Quantum Feature Extraction

The core of our feature engineering lies in the Quantum Fourier Transform (QFT). Unlike classical Fourier methods, QFT operates in an exponentially larger Hilbert space, allowing it to potentially identify subtle, non-linear periodicities in market data that classical methods might miss.

  • Amplitude Encoding: Normalized time-series data points are encoded into the amplitudes of a quantum state vector.
  • Frequency Analysis: The QFT circuit transforms this time-domain state into the frequency domain.
  • Feature Vector: The measurement probabilities of the resulting quantum state form the feature vector, representing the strength of various market periodicities.

The VQC Model

The Variational Quantum Classifier (VQC) is a hybrid quantum-classical algorithm well-suited for noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) devices.

Fig 2: A conceptual representation of the parameterized quantum circuit (ansatz) used in the VQC.

Training Process:

  • The VQC consists of a parameterized quantum circuit (ansatz) that is optimized using a classical computer.
  • The QFT-derived features are encoded into the quantum circuit.
  • The circuit's parameters (rotation angles of quantum gates) are iteratively updated by a classical optimizer (e.g., SPSA, Adam) to minimize a cost function that measures the difference between predicted and actual market labels.

Results & Discussion

The model was trained on FTSE 100 data from 2010-2020 and tested on data from 2021-2023. The VQC, trained on QFT features, was benchmarked against a classical Support Vector Machine (SVM) model trained on standard technical indicators.

Fig 3: Prediction accuracy of the QML model compared to the classical SVM benchmark.

  • Accuracy: The VQC model achieved a predictive accuracy of 58.7% on the test set, outperforming the classical SVM's 53.2%.
  • Insights: The superior performance suggests that QFT successfully extracts features relevant to market prediction that are not captured by classical indicators.
  • Challenges: The primary limitations were the noise in current quantum hardware and the computational cost of simulation. As quantum hardware improves, we expect the model's performance to increase significantly.

Conclusion & Future Work

Our work demonstrates the promising potential of hybrid quantum-classical models in financial forecasting. By using QFT for feature engineering, we created a feature set that enabled a Variational Quantum Classifier to achieve higher predictive accuracy than a standard classical model.

Future Directions:

  • Explore more advanced quantum feature maps.
  • Implement quantum noise mitigation techniques.
  • Scale the model to larger datasets and different financial instruments.
